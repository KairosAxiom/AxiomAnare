# Why bearing faults are not dominant peaks in the raw spectrum

A rolling-element bearing defect is an impulsive, transient event: each time a rolling
element strikes a localised fault (spall) on a raceway or on itself, it produces a
brief, broadband impact. These impacts repeat at the bearing's characteristic defect
frequency (BPFO, BPFI, BSF, or FTF).

In the raw velocity spectrum this impact energy is spread thinly across a wide band and
is almost never a dominant peak — it is routinely buried beneath the shaft-synchronous
lines (1×, 2×). Diagnosing bearings by ranking the tallest peaks in the raw velocity
spectrum therefore fails: shaft-synchronous faults (unbalance at 1×, misalignment at
2×/3×) win by construction. Bearing faults require dedicated methods that recover the
repetition rate of the impulses rather than their raw spectral amplitude — namely the
time-domain impulsiveness metrics and envelope demodulation.
